# Social proof

**Memory hook:** people follow people.

## The idea
When people aren't sure, they look at what others like them did. A
product, plan or feature that many people chose and rated well feels
safer than one nobody seems to use. Being "the first to try it" feels
risky; joining hundreds of others doesn't. So show real evidence of
other people's choices, right where the user is deciding.

## Why it works
Following the crowd is a shortcut the brain uses when it lacks
information: if many people chose something and were happy, it's
probably fine. A visible status label ("Top rated", "New") also colors
how people see everything else about the item, before they read the
details (the video calls this the halo effect).
(The source claims a large jump in sales from these changes; it shows
no data, so treat the numbers as an illustration.)

## Before / after
- **Before:** an item page with a title, a price and a button. Nothing
  says whether anyone else has chosen it.
- **After:** right next to the title, the real rating and the real
  number of reviews ("4.9 ★ · 221 reviews"), and if it's true, a calm
  line with recent real activity ("Chosen by 512 people this week").
- **Before:** a plan picker with three equal-looking options.
- **After:** the plan most users actually pick carries a small
  "Most chosen" label.

## Where it applies
- **Items and content:** ratings, review counts, how many people saved
  or used it.
- **Plans and options:** label the option most people really choose.
- **Features:** "Used by 80% of people who track expenses" to encourage
  trying a setting, if the figure is real.
- **Empty or new things:** when there's no proof yet, say it's new
  instead of faking popularity.

## Do
- Show exact, real figures ("221 reviews", not "200+"), next to the
  thing they describe (see placement.md: trust next to what it confirms).
- Keep each label short and calm.

## Don't
- Stack many badges and counters until the item looks like an ad.
- Add urgency decorations (fire icons, flashing counters) to social proof.

## Guardrail (strict)
- Every number, rating and label ("Best seller", "Most popular",
  "Top rated") must be real, current and based on actual data. Fake or
  inflated social proof is illegal under EU consumer law, and users
  stop trusting the whole app once they spot it.
- Reviews are shown as they are: no hiding negative ones to raise the
  average.
- A "most popular" label marks what people choose; it never comes with
  that option pre-selected when it costs money.
Note: the source pairs "500+ sold this week" with a fire icon, and
advises choosing specific-looking numbers because they feel authentic.
This repo shows only real figures and drops the urgency decoration.
