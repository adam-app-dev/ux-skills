# Show the catch (transparency)

**Memory hook:** say the catch before they find it.

## The idea
Every offer has a catch: a charge after the trial, a cancellation
deadline, a fee, a limit. Users know this and go looking for it. When the
screen hides it, they assume the worst and hesitate. When the screen
names it openly, and even promises to help with it, the user stops
searching and starts trusting. Answer the biggest worry right next to the
action, before the user has to ask.

## Why it works
When a company points out a downside on its own, people read it as
"they're on my side", and trust goes up rather than down. Uncertainty is
also a cost in itself: a user who wonders "will there be hidden fees on
the next screen?" is already halfway to leaving.
(The video calls this "transparency bias"; treat the name as an
illustration, not an established fact.)

## Before / after
- **Before:** a free-trial screen that mentions the price once and says
  nothing about when the charge happens.
- **After:** a short timeline: today you get full access; two days before
  the trial ends, we remind you; on the last day the first charge happens,
  and you can cancel before then. The reminder line does more for trust
  than any feature list.
- **Before:** a booking button that just says "Reserve".
- **After:** the button shows the full total, and a line underneath
  states the free-cancellation date. The two most common worries
  ("how much really?", "what if my plans change?") are answered before
  the tap.

## Where it applies
- **Trials and subscriptions:** when the charge happens, how much, and
  how to cancel; offer a reminder before the first charge.
- **Bookings and orders:** the full total including fees, and the
  cancellation or refund terms.
- **Permissions and data:** what is collected, and what is not.
- **Destructive or irreversible actions:** what can and can't be undone
  (pairs with loss-framing.md).
- **Limits:** state free-tier limits upfront, not at the moment they're hit.
- **Plan choices:** put the key terms ("save 15%", "cancel anytime")
  inside each option card, so the reassurance arrives at the moment of
  choosing.
- **Trust badges:** near the main button, answer the worry this audience
  actually has ("independently tested", "60-day refund"), not generic
  promises every app lists.

## Do
- Find the single biggest worry for this action and answer it next to
  the button.
- Use real dates and real totals ("cancel before 26 March", "€445 total").
- Offer to remind the user before anything costs them money.
- Ask "what is this specific audience afraid of?" and answer that.

## Don't
- Reveal fees, charges or terms only on the last screen.
- Bury the catch in small, faded text far from the action.

## Guardrail
Every promise must be kept: if the screen says "we'll remind you", the
reminder must really be sent. Transparency is the goal, not a tactic to
seem trustworthy while hiding something else. Trust badges must be true
and checkable (a real test, a real refund policy).

Sources: uxpeak, three A/B test examples (paywall, ride-hailing, booking);
