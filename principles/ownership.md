# Ownership (IKEA effect and endowment effect)

**Memory hook:** what I built or chose is mine.

## The idea
Two related effects:
- **IKEA effect:** people value what they built themselves more than an
  identical thing someone else made. Assembled IKEA furniture "looks pretty
  good" to the person who spent the afternoon building it.
- **Endowment effect:** you don't even need to build anything. Just
  *feeling* that something is yours is enough to value it more.

A plain sign-up screen (email, password, button) contains nothing that
belongs to the user, so closing the tab costs nothing. Once the user has
made choices, leaving feels like abandoning something they made.

## Why it works
Effort and choice create attachment. People protect what they consider
theirs and are reluctant to throw away time they've invested.

## Before / after
- **Before:** a sign-up page with email, password and "Sign up". Nothing
  on it is the user's.
- **After:** before signing up, the user is already building: choosing a
  name, a title, a color palette, a card style. The button says
  "Continue", because by now leaving would mean abandoning their creation.
- **Real-world example:** Duolingo. Before creating an account you pick a
  language, set a daily goal and finish your first lesson. By the time the
  sign-up screen appears you've invested about 10 minutes, and you won't
  throw that away.

## Where it applies
- **First use:** let users create something (a profile, an item, a first
  entry) before formalities.
- **Home screens and dashboards:** let users choose or order what they see.
- **Content:** names, photos, colors, icons the user picks for their own items.
- **Plans and goals:** let users set their own target instead of a fixed one.
- **Wording:** label things as theirs ("Your plan", their item's name),
  and after building, the button says "Continue", not "Sign up".

## Do
- Offer a few meaningful choices early.
- Show the user's choices back to them (their name, their photo) on later screens.

## Don't
- Start with an empty form that contains nothing of the user's.
- Offer so many customization options that it turns into decision fatigue
  (balance with smart-defaults.md: pre-fill, let them adjust).
- Mix "my" and "your" on the same screen. First-person button text
  ("Start my free trial") is one option; "Your plan" is another. Pick one
  voice per app and use it everywhere. (A later source claims "my" on a
  button creates ownership; treat that as untested, not proven.)

## Guardrail
Choices must be useful to the user, not busywork to trap them.
Leaving, exporting or deleting must stay easy.
