# Loss framing

**Memory hook:** losing hurts twice as much as gaining feels good.

## The idea
Daniel Kahneman's research showed that the pain of losing something is
psychologically about twice as strong as the pleasure of gaining the same
thing. So every time a feature is framed as something to gain, the weaker
motivator is being used. Flip it: show what the user will lose if they
don't act.

## Why it works
**Status quo bias:** people are wired to protect what they already have.
A list of benefits has no weight; a specific thing they're about to lose
does. Make the cost of doing nothing visible.

## Before / after
- **Before:** a storage app's upgrade screen: nice icon, feature list,
  "Upgrade now", and an effortless "Maybe later". Nothing is at stake,
  so the screen has zero weight.
- **After:** the same goal, but the screen shows the user's actual files,
  by name, that will be affected when they run out of space.
  The stake is concrete.

## Where it applies
- **Destructive actions:** deleting, resetting or leaving names exactly
  what goes away ("You'll lose 2 years of records"), not just "Delete?".
- **Turning things off:** disabling a reminder or sync names what will
  stop happening.
- **Expiring things:** subscriptions, trials or storage show the specific
  items affected.
- **Unsaved work:** leaving a screen with changes names what will be lost.
- **Limits and upgrades:** when a limit is reached, show the user's actual
  items that are affected.

## Do
- Be specific: real item names, real dates, real counts.
- Use it to protect users from mistakes as much as to drive action.

## Don't
- Use vague gains ("Unlock more features!") when a concrete stake exists.

## Guardrail (strict)
This principle is the easiest to abuse.
- Only show losses that are real and accurate.
- Show a deadline only if it truly exists; never a fake countdown.
- The dismiss option stays neutral: "Not now" or "Cancel",
  never shaming ("I'll risk it").
Note: the source video's example uses a countdown and an "I'll risk it"
button. This repo deliberately keeps the real-loss idea and drops those
two tactics.
